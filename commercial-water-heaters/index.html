When to Call for Commercial Water Heaters

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Loud sediment noise from equipment that runs all day
  • Energy costs climbing while usage stays flat
  • An aging unit you would rather replace on your schedule than in an emergency
  • Trouble reaching sanitation temperatures in the kitchen
  • Lukewarm water at the exact time you need the most
  • Rust-colored water, leaks at fittings, or moisture under the heater
  • A recirculation loop that no longer seems to do its job
  • Unsteady delivery temperatures that suggest a failing mixing valve

A Slow Leak Never Stays Slow

A worn-out anode rod, a sediment-choked tank, or a weak thermocouple rarely fixes itself, and the repair usually costs more the longer it waits. If you notice any of these signs, contact Atlanta Water Heater at (470) 200-7307 for a prompt inspection.

Why Hire a Professional for Commercial Water Heaters?

Commercial water heaters operate at a different scale. Gas input is often several times that of a home unit, which affects gas line sizing, meter capacity, combustion air, and venting. Electric models can draw enough current to require serious service capacity. Storage often runs at 140 degrees or higher, so thermostatic mixing valves are needed to deliver safe water at fixtures, and those valves must be matched to the flow and tested. Recirculation loops need the right pump, working check valves, and balancing, or hot water takes the easy path and distant fixtures stay cold.

Code compliance and scheduling matter just as much. Permits and inspections are standard for commercial work, larger tanks may carry extra requirements, and a failed inspection can keep a kitchen or laundromat closed. Atlanta Water Heater handles that coordination up front, plans installations for early mornings, evenings, or closed days, and replaces banked units one at a time whenever the layout allows. The result is a system sized for your peak, installed correctly, and brought online with as little disruption to your customers as possible.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Here's where to begin:

  • Record fixture temperatures regularly, especially before inspections
  • Schedule upgrades during slow periods instead of waiting for a breakdown
  • Keep the area around the heaters clear for combustion air and service access
  • Set a flushing schedule based on usage; busy tanks collect sediment fast
  • Replace consumed anode rods before the tank itself starts to corrode
  • Have mixing valves checked so fixtures stay neither scalding nor lukewarm
  • Listen for a failing recirculation pump and have check valves inspected
